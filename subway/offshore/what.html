Types of leads you can report

The Offshore Tax Informant Program (OTIP) wants to know about international tax non-compliance such as:

  • individuals failing to report foreign income on their Canadian tax return
  • individuals or corporations using complex tax structures to avoid paying the taxes they owe in Canada
  • non-residents who fail to pay tax on income earned, or received from sources, in Canada

Examples

Foreign assets and foreign income
  • A Canadian resident taxpayer does not report to the CRA (Canada Revenue Agency) inherited assets located outside of Canada or the income earned on those assets
  • A Canadian resident taxpayer does not report offshore assets they obtained before becoming a Canadian resident or the income earned on the assets
  • A taxpayer holds substantial unreported assets through offshore corporations, which provides cash flow to the taxpayer
  • A Canadian taxpayer owns properties in a foreign country that they earned rental income from but do not report that income to the CRA
  • A Canadian taxpayer has offshore assets but does not report income earned on the assets to the CRA
Corporations
  • A Canadian company overstates its expenses by asking a foreign supplier to inflate invoices for services rendered. The supplier takes a small fee for this service and transfers the difference to an offshore bank account for the Canadian company
  • A Canadian company offers customers considerable discounts if they pay part of the invoice in cash. The company then moves the unreported business income offshore
Lifestyle
  • A Canadian resident’s income is low and not consistent with their lifestyle. They live in a high-value home but declare very little income to the CRA.
Non-residents of Canada
  • A non-resident receives royalty, dividends, or interest payments from a Canadian source where tax was not withheld at source
  • A non-resident earns rental income from properties owned in Canada but did not report it to the CRA
  • A non-resident is paid for film or video acting services performed in Canada but the incorrect amount of non-resident tax was withheld by the Canadian payer
  • A non-resident earns income from employment in Canada or from a business carried on in Canada but does not report it to the CRA

This list is not exhaustive. Other situations and arrangements involving international tax non-compliance may be eligible under the OTIP.
