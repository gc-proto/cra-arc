After you submit a lead

Steps

  1. We evaluate an informant's submission

    The Offshore Tax Informant Program (OTIP) conducts a preliminary review of your submission to

    • verify the identity of the Canadian taxpayers (individuals, corporations, or trusts) that are the subjects of your submission
    • determine if your submission includes sufficient, specific, and credible facts about  the alleged international tax non-compliance
    • verify that the information you provided:
      • is about international tax non-compliance
      • shows that the potential federal tax owing, excluding interest and penalties, is $100,000 or more
    • ensure the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) does not already have the Canadian taxpayer(s) under compliance action or have the information in its possession
    • determines whether to share the information with CRA audit programs other CRA program areas for other possible enforcement-related actions

  2. Determine reward eligibility

    If you express interest in being considered for a reward, the OTIP verifies:

    • your identify, as the informant
    • that  you meet the eligibility criteria. See Eligibility for more details.

    Case is not accepted in the program

    If a case is not accepted in the reward program, the OTIP will notify you by mail.

    Case is accepted in the program

    If a case is recommended for inclusion in the reward program, it is sent to an internal committee of senior management representatives for approval to enter into a contract.

    If the CRA will recover at least $100,000 in federal tax

    When it is not apparent that the CRA will recover at least $100,000 in federal tax, the OTIP will not offer you a contract but will keep the information on file. If an audit does take place and the CRA reassesses and collects at least $100,000 in federal tax, the OTIP will reach out to the informant to offer a contract if all of the conditions of the program are met. Therefore, it is important to keep your address up to date with the OTIP. 

  3. Enter into a contract

    After receiving approval from the internal committee, the OTIP will enter into a contract with the informant. Depending on the situation, this can happen before or after an audit.

    The contract sets out the terms and conditions under which the informant provides information to the CRA. In exchange for that information, the CRA agrees to pay a reward to the informant if all the terms and conditions in the contract are met.

    Example of contract timing after an audit George knows that his business partner is hiding his personal assets in a foreign country. George has emails, property records, and bank statements to prove it. He completes Form RC626 and sends it to the OTIP along with the supporting documents.

    The OTIP reviews the allegations and documents George provided and estimates that the amount owing to the CRA is just under the $100,000 threshold. As a result, George is not offered a contract by the CRA. The OTIP does refer the information received to an audit program for compliance action. The CRA conducts an audit of the taxpayer George identified and discovers that the taxpayer actually owes more than $300,000 in federal tax to the CRA.

    It has been several years since George sent in his submission, but George now qualifies for a contract. George kept the OTIP updated with his current address so the OTIP was able to reach out to him to offer him a contract for the information he provided.

    After the taxpayer’s appeals rights have expired and the CRA has collected the taxes owing, the OTIP will issue a reward payment to George.

    If you have information similar to George’s, or other information about major international tax non-compliance, you could receive a financial reward. To find out if you could be eligible go to Eligibility.

  4. Understand how long the process takes

    The CRA works to conclude the process as efficiently as possible. However, it may take several years until the CRA audits, assesses the additional federal tax, the taxpayer's objection and appeal rights expire, and the CRA collects the amount owing.

  5. Inform you of your right of redress

    The contract provides a dispute resolution process for informants. If an informant disagrees with a decision made under the terms of the contract, an internal review panel comprised of senior management representatives will revie the matter. The panel will notify the informant and the OTIP of its  decision in writing.

  6. Receive information about the status of your case

    To ensure confidentiality, a request for an update on a case must be made in writing to the OTIP and refer to your case number. Because the Income Tax Act contains a general prohibition against the disclosure of taxpayer information, the OTIP can only tell you the status (case is open or closed) and disposition (the final outcome) of your particular case. The OTIP will not share any information about the taxpayer or potential audit with you.
    You will receive correspondence by mail from the OTIP only at your designated mailing address to

    • acknowledge receipt of the submission
    • initiate contract discussions
    • inform you of a payment under the contract
    • serve notice of contract termination or completion

  7. Confidentiality of taxpayer information

    The information you provide is collected under the authority of federal tax laws, and it is protected under the confidentiality provisions of those laws, as well as by privacy laws that impose strict limits on what the CRA can disclose. This means that you will not receive feedback or updates on the information that you provide. The CRA may use the information you provide to make sure taxpayers meet their tax obligations
