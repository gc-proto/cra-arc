The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is committed to making it easier for First Nations, Inuit, and Métis Peoples to file their taxes and receive the benefit and credit payments they are entitled to.

This form is also known as:

"Let us help you get your benefits!" Indigenous credit and benefit short return

On this page

  • Why you should file
  • How to file
  • Related resources

Why you should file

To make sure you keep getting your benefit and credit payments, you need to do your taxes every year even if:

  • your income is tax exempt under section 87 of the Indian Act
  • you have no income at all
  • you are married or in a common-law relationship, both you and your partner need to file a tax return

Payments you may receive when you file your return

  • All individuals  

    • Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB)
    • Canada child benefit (CCB)
    • Canada workers benefit (CWB)

  • Additional payments based on where you live

    Alberta benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the:

    • Alberta child and family benefit
    British Columbia benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefit and refundable tax credits:

    • BC family benefit
    • Sales tax credit
    • Home renovation tax credit for seniors and persons with disabilities
    • British Columbia renter’s tax credit
    Manitoba benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following refundable tax credits and rebate:

    • Personal tax credit
    • Homeowners affordability tax credit
    • Renters affordability tax credit
    • Seniors’ school tax rebate
    • Primary caregiver tax credit
    New Brunswick benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefit and refundable tax credits:

    • New Brunswick child tax benefit
    • New Brunswick harmonized sales tax credit
    • New Brunswick seniors’ home renovation tax credit
    Newfoundland and Labrador benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefits and refundable tax credit:

    • Newfoundland and Labrador child benefit (NLCB) and Early childhood nutrition supplement (ECNS)
    • Newfoundland and Labrador income supplement
    • Newfoundland and Labrador seniors’ benefit
    • Newfoundland and Labrador disability benefit
    • Physical activity tax credit
    Northwest Territories benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefit and refundable tax credit:

    • Northwest Territories child benefit
    • Cost of living tax credit
    Nova Scotia benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefit and refundable tax credits:

    • Nova Scotia child benefit
    • Nova Scotia affordable living tax credit
    • Volunteer firefighters and ground search and rescue tax credit
    • Children’s sports and arts tax credit
    Nunavut benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefit and refundable tax credit:

    • Nunavut child benefit
    • Cost of living tax credit
    Ontario benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefits and refundable tax credits:

    • Ontario child benefit
    • Ontario senior homeowners’ property tax grant
    • Ontario seniors’ public transit tax credit
    • Ontario trillium benefit (OTB)
      • Ontario sales tax credit
      • Ontario energy and property tax credit
      • Northern Ontario energy credit
    Prince Edward Island benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefit and refundable tax credits:

    • Prince Edward Island child benefit
    • Prince Edward Island sales tax credit
    • Prince Edward Island volunteer firefighter and volunteer search and rescue personnel tax credit
    Quebec benefits and credits

    There are no additional benefits, credits, or deductions that can be claimed using SimpleFile.

    Saskatchewan benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefits and tax credit:

    • Saskatchewan low-income tax credit
    • Senior supplementary amount
    • Active families benefit
    Yukon benefits and credits

    You may also be eligible for the following benefit and tax credit:

    • Yukon child benefit
    • Children’s fitness tax credit

How to file

Consider there are alternate ways to file:

  • Most individuals can complete their tax return using certified tax software, and don't need to use this form.
  • Many individuals might qualify alternate free tax filing options provided by the CRA:
    • Pre-filled tax return in your CRA account
    • SimpleFile
    • Free tax clinics

If you choose to use this paper form, follow the steps below.

    • Identify as a First Nation, Inuk or Métis
    • Be a resident of Canada
    • Not be bankrupt
    • Have a taxable income in:

  2. Get the form

    Download the form based on which province or territory you resided in on December 31 for the tax year you are filing for.

    • Alberta
    • British Columbia
    • Manitoba
    • New Brunswick
    • Newfoundland and Labrador
    • Nova Scotia
    • Northwest Territories
    • Nunavut
    • Ontario
    • Prince Edward Island
    • Quebec
    • Saskatchewan
    • Yukon

  3. Complete your return

    1. Enter personal information

      Fill in your name, address, and Social Insurance Number (SIN) or other tax number.

    2. Report income

      Use your tax slips to report all income, including:

      • Employment and self-employment income
      • Income from assets
      • Tips and other earnings

      Check if all information is included

      You should receive most tax slips by the end of February.

      If a tax slip is missing, contact the issuer of the slip for a copy.

    3. Claim deductions, credits, and expenses

      Claim eligible deductions, credits, and expenses to reduce the amount of tax you owe.

      This includes:

      • Basic personal amount
      • Northern residents deduction
      • Disability tax credit (DTC)
      • [Tuition expenses]
      • [link]

    4. Review your return

      Make sure all information is true, accurate, and complete.

      If needed, you can update some of your personal information on your tax return, including your:

      • Email address
      • Marital status
      • Language of correspondence

  4. Send your return

    • We will not accept your return if you directly mail it to us
    • We will only accept your return if it has been submitted by a band council office or friendship centre. They send your return to:

      Winnipeg Tax Centre
      Post Office Box 14001
      Station Main Winnipeg MB
      R3C 3M3

  5. After you file

    There are 3 possible outcomes from your tax return:

    • Get a refund
    • Have a balance owing
    • Have no refund or balance owing

    The CRA will send you a notice of assessment (NOA) with the outcome of your tax return.

    Your NOA and any refund are usually processed within 12 weeks.
