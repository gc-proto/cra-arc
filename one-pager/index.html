The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is committed to making it easier for First Nations, Inuit, and Métis Peoples to file their taxes and receive the benefit and credit payments they are entitled to.

This is only avaliable as a paper form. It is also also known as:

"Let us help you get your benefits!" Indigenous credit and benefit short return

Do your taxes to get payments

To make sure you keep getting your benefit and credit payments, you need to do your taxes every year even if:

  • You have no income at all
  • Your income is tax exempt under section 87 of the Indian Act

If you are married or in a common-law relationship, both you and your partner need to file a tax return in order to receive a payment.

Payments you may be eligible for

  • Federal payments

    • Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB)
    • Canada child benefit (CCB)
    • Canada workers benefit (CWB)
    • Children's special allowances (CSA)
    • Child disability benefit (CDB)

  • Provincial and territorial payments

    Based on where you live, you may also get the:

    • Alberta child and family benefit
    • BC family benefit
    • Ontario child benefit
    • Ontario senior homeowners’ property tax grant
    • Ontario trillium benefit
    • Prince Edward Island sales tax credit
    • New Brunswick child tax benefit
    • New Brunswick harmonized sales tax credit
    • Newfoundland and Labrador child benefit (and Early childhood nutrition supplement)
    • Newfoundland and Labrador income supplement
    • Newfoundland and Labrador disability amount
    • Newfoundland and Labrador disability benefit
    • Newfoundland and Labrador seniors' benefit
    • Northwest Territories child benefit
    • Nova Scotia affordable living tax credit
    • Nova Scotia child benefit
    • Nunavut child benefit
    • Prince Edward Island child benefit
    • Saskatchewan low-income tax credit
    • Yukon child benefit

      • Free tax clinics
      • T1 income tax package

  3. Get the form

    Download the form based on which province or territory you resided in on December 31 for the tax year you are filing for.

    • Nunavut
    • Ontario
    • Prince Edward Island
    • Quebec
    • Saskatchewan
    • Yukon

  4. Complete your return

    1. Enter personal information

      Fill in your name, address, and Social Insurance Number (SIN) or other tax number.

    2. Report income

      Use your tax slips to report all income, including:

      • Employment and self-employment income
      • Income from assets
      • Tips and other earnings

      Check if all information is included

      You should receive most tax slips by the end of February.

      If a tax slip is missing, contact the issuer of the slip for a copy.

    3. Claim deductions, credits, and expenses

      Claim eligible deductions, credits, and expenses to reduce the amount of tax you owe.

      This includes:

      • Basic personal amount
      • Northern residents deduction
      • Disability tax credit (DTC)
      • [Tuition expenses]
      • [link]

    4. Review your return

      Make sure all information is true, accurate, and complete.

      If needed, you can update some of your personal information on your tax return, including your:

      • Email address
      • Marital status
      • Language of correspondence

  5. Send your return

    • We will not accept your short if you mail it directly to us.
    • We will only accept your return if it has been submitted by a band council office or friendship centre. They send your return to:

      Winnipeg Tax Centre
      Post Office Box 14001
      Station Main Winnipeg MB
      R3C 3M3

  6. After you file

    There are 3 possible outcomes from your tax return:

    • Get a refund
    • Have a balance owing
    • Have no refund or balance owing

    The CRA will send you a notice of assessment (NOA) with the outcome of your tax return.

    Your NOA and any refund are usually processed within 12 weeks.
